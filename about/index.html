About

A Route 66 lover behind the screen.

Route 66 Logbook is a one-person studio. Which mostly means me, the road, and a lot of late nights.

Hi, I'm Carole. I'm French, and for as long as I can remember I said I would go to the US. It was my childhood dream. Somewhere inside that dream there was always one road: Route 66. The Mother Road. A diner in the middle of nowhere, a neon sign, the desert opening up ahead.

I first drove a piece of it in 2017, part of a long loop that took me through 35 states and more than 51,000 km, around 31,700 miles. We did a little of 66 back then, Illinois especially, properly. To follow the original alignment I downloaded a map a stranger had made a couple of years earlier. By the time I got there, so much of it was already gone. Diners closed. Motels boarded up. Whole stops that simply weren't there anymore. It felt like the Great Barrier Reef: if you don't go now, what's left will be gone in a few years.

But every place that was still standing felt like a small miracle. A milkshake at a counter that had been serving them since the 1950s. A corn dog at a spot that had been frying them since the 1940s. Real people keeping a piece of history alive against the chains and the freeways. Even with everything else I was seeing, I knew Route 66 deserved a trip of its own one day, the full road, end to end.

In March 2026 I went back to do exactly that, Los Angeles to Chicago, the whole route this time. I made sure I had the latest guides, the 2025 editions, and I still hit the same wall, worse. The information is patchy, out of date, scattered across forums, old blogs and maps nobody has touched in years. You arrive somewhere full of excitement and find a padlock and a faded sign.

“
There is never a definitive guide to Route 66. It simply isn't possible. The road is alive, it changes all the time. So instead of one more guide that's out of date the day it's printed, I wanted to build a living one.

So I made this.

Route 66 Logbook is the app I wish I'd had on both trips. A living map of the road, stop by stop, showing what is actually still there: what's open, what's closed, what's gone. I'm a designer, not a tech company. I built it on my own, out of love for the road, and the unglamorous part was the data. Hundreds of stops researched and checked one by one, because outdated information is the whole problem I was trying to solve.

The Route 66 community is powerful and passionate, and the app is really built for it. You can flag what you find on the road, when something has closed, changed or disappeared, so the map stays current for whoever drives it next. And you can log your own trip as you go. With Route 66 turning 100, there is a flood of paper passports and stamp books to keep track of, so I wanted one simple place to record your journey: your own logbook of the Mother Road.

I'm not here to sell you a perfect product. I'm a traveller who got tired of arriving too late, and decided to do something about it. If it helps one more person catch a diner before it closes for good, it was worth every late night.

See you on the road.
